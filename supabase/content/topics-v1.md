# Offhand — Topic pool v1

125 topics, 25 per category.

Each topic is followed by its core in short form — usually the common misconception or the
surprising point. That is the reason the topic is in the pool.

Edit this file and run `npm run db:seed` to regenerate the seed migration. Do not hand-edit

## 1. Mind & Behaviour

1. **Dunning-Kruger effect** — A little knowledge produces a lot of confidence.
2. **The Monty Hall problem** — Switching doubles your odds. Almost nobody believes it.
3. **The birthday paradox** — With 23 people, a shared birthday is more likely than not.
4. **The bystander effect** — The more witnesses there are, the less likely anyone helps.
5. **Sunk cost fallacy** — Throwing good money after bad because you already paid.
6. **Confirmation bias** — We go looking for evidence of what we already believe.
7. **The halo effect** — Attractive people are assumed to be more competent as well.
8. **Placebo and nocebo** — Expecting side effects is enough to produce them.
9. **The cocktail party effect** — Your own name cuts through any amount of noise.
10. **The spotlight effect** — Nobody noticed the stain on your shirt. Truly nobody.
11. **Survivorship bias** — The planes that did not come back had the bullet holes that mattered.
12. **Anchoring** — An arbitrary number beforehand shifts every estimate that follows.
13. **The Zeigarnik effect** — Unfinished tasks stay in your head better than finished ones.
14. **The hedonic treadmill** — After every gain in happiness you return to where you started.
15. **The prisoner's dilemma** — Two rational choices add up to the worst possible outcome.
16. **Simpson's paradox** — A trend reverses when you merge the groups.
17. **Benford's law** — In real-world numbers, 1 leads far more often than 9. Used to catch fraud.
18. **Regression to the mean** — Why praise "does not work" and criticism appears to.
19. **The Pygmalion effect** — Other people's expectations change actual performance.
20. **The peak-end rule** — We judge experiences by their peak and their ending, not their length.
21. **Choice blindness** — People confidently justify decisions they never made.
22. **Dunbar's number** — Around 150 stable relationships is the cognitive ceiling.
23. **Semantic satiation** — A word repeated often enough stops meaning anything.
24. **Base rate neglect** — Why a positive test for a rare disease is usually wrong.
25. **The decoy effect** — A third, worse option changes how you choose between the first two.

## 2. Universe & Physics

1. **The Fermi paradox** — The universe is vast and old. So where is everyone?
2. **Time dilation** — Moving clocks run slow. Measured, not theorised.
3. **Why astronauts float** — Not because gravity is missing, but because they are permanently falling.
4. **Tidal locking** — Why the Moon always shows us the same face.
5. **Olbers' paradox** — If there are infinitely many stars, why is the night sky dark?
6. **Quantum entanglement** — Two particles correlate across any distance without sending information.
7. **Lagrange points** — Places in space where a satellite stays put. The James Webb telescope hangs at one.
8. **Redshift** — How we know the universe is expanding.
9. **The cosmic microwave background** — The echo of the Big Bang, visible in the static of old televisions.
10. **The light year** — A distance, not a duration. Astronomy's most common misunderstanding.
11. **Dark matter** — 85 per cent of the matter in the universe, and nobody knows what it is.
12. **Heisenberg's uncertainty principle** — Not a limit of measurement, but a property of nature.
13. **Entropy** — Why time only runs in one direction.
14. **Is space cold?** — A vacuum barely has a temperature. Freezing is not your main problem out there.
15. **The Doppler effect** — Why an ambulance changes pitch as it passes you.
16. **Kessler syndrome** — A chain reaction of orbital debris could make orbit unusable.
17. **Why rockets do not fly straight up** — You reach orbit through sideways speed, not altitude.
18. **The Roche limit** — Why moons break apart and turn into rings.
19. **Antimatter** — It exists, we manufacture it, and nobody knows why there is so little of it.
20. **The twin paradox** — The travelling twin comes back younger.
21. **Where heavy elements come from** — The iron in your blood was made in a supernova.
22. **Schrödinger's cat** — Originally meant as criticism, today usually misquoted.
23. **The Planck length** — The smallest distance it still makes sense to describe.
24. **Solar sails and light pressure** — Light has no rest mass but still exerts pressure.
25. **Neutrinos** — Billions pass through you every second without touching anything.

## 3. Earth & Life

1. **Endosymbiotic theory** — Your mitochondria were once free-living bacteria.
2. **Trees are made of air** — A tree's mass comes from CO2 in the air, not from the soil.
3. **The Amazon is not the lungs of the Earth** — It consumes almost as much oxygen as it produces.
4. **Mycorrhizal networks** — Fungi connect trees underground into a trading system.
5. **Why chilli tastes hot** — Capsaicin fools heat receptors. Spiciness is not a taste.
6. **Convergent evolution** — Why dolphins and sharks look alike despite not being related.
7. **Why water is densest at 4 degrees** — It is why lakes freeze from the top and the life in them survives.
8. **The Mpemba effect** — Under the right conditions, hot water freezes faster than cold.
9. **Tardigrades** — They survive vacuum, radiation and decades without water.
10. **Petrichor** — The smell of rain comes from bacteria in the soil.
11. **Clouds weigh tonnes** — An average cumulus weighs as much as 100 elephants and floats anyway.
12. **The square-cube law** — Why elephants cannot jump and giant spiders are impossible.
13. **Epigenetics** — The environment can change gene activity across generations.
14. **Why antibiotics do not work on viruses** — Probably the most consequential everyday error in medicine.
15. **The Portuguese man o' war** — Not one animal but a colony of specialised organisms.
16. **Why stars twinkle and planets do not** — A point source against a disc.
17. **Bioluminescence** — Why deep-sea creatures glow and how they do it chemically.
18. **Birds flying in V formation** — Aerodynamics, not sociability.
19. **The eel** — Nobody has ever observed one reproducing in the wild.
20. **Extremophiles at black smokers** — Life without sunlight, running entirely on chemistry.
21. **The foehn wind** — Why a wind brings rain on one side of a mountain and warmth on the other.
22. **Plate tectonics** — The Himalayas are still growing, centimetres every year.
23. **Karst and sinkholes** — How water dissolves rock until the ground suddenly gives way.
24. **There is no such thing as cold-blooded** — Ectotherms are often warmer than you are.
25. **Why leaves change colour** — The colours were there all along, just hidden by chlorophyll.

## 4. History

1. **Vikings did not wear horned helmets** — The invention comes from an opera in 1876.
2. **The potato changed Europe** — It ended famines and made population growth possible.
3. **The Emu War** — In 1932 Australia sent the military against flightless birds and lost.
4. **The Library of Alexandria** — No single great fire, but centuries of decline.
5. **Tulip mania** — The first documented speculative bubble, Holland 1637.
6. **The year without a summer** — A volcanic eruption in 1815 caused crop failures worldwide and snow in June.
7. **The Silk Road was not a road** — It was a loose network, and the name was only coined in 1877.
8. **The Vasa** — The prestige warship sank in 1628 after 1,300 metres.
9. **The Little Ice Age** — Centuries of colder climate, with consequences reaching into wars and witch trials.
10. **The "Dark Ages"** — A polemical term from the Renaissance, historically hard to defend.
11. **Napoleon was not short** — Average height. The myth comes from British propaganda and units of measurement.
12. **The Spanish flu did not come from Spain** — Spain simply did not censor the news.
13. **The Bronze Age collapse** — Several advanced civilisations fell almost simultaneously around 1200 BC.
14. **Stanislav Petrov** — In 1983 an officer ignored a nuclear alert and probably prevented a world war.
15. **The Hanseatic League** — A league of cities without a state that controlled northern European trade for centuries.
16. **The fall of the Aztecs** — Disease killed far more people than weapons did.
17. **Easter Island** — The popular collapse narrative is now heavily disputed.
18. **The longitude problem** — A clockmaker solved the greatest navigation problem in seafaring.
19. **The Battle of Cannae** — How an outnumbered army destroyed a larger one by encirclement.
20. **The Berlin Conference** — Why Africa's borders are so straight.
21. **Witch hunts were early modern** — Their peak came after the Middle Ages, not during them.
22. **The Terracotta Army** — 8,000 figures, each with its own face, found by a man digging a well.
23. **The Black Death** — Whether rats were really the main carrier is disputed.
24. **The Zimmermann telegram** — A decrypted message pulled the United States into the First World War.
25. **The Mercator projection** — Why Greenland looks as big as Africa on maps despite being 14 times smaller.

## 5. Technology

1. **Why the keyboard is laid out that way** — The usual story about jamming typebars is disputed.
2. **The Streisand effect** — Trying to delete something is what spreads it.
3. **The Y2K bug** — That nothing happened was the result of years of work, not proof of panic-mongering.
4. **Moore's law** — Not a law of nature but an observation that became a target.
5. **GPS needs relativity** — Without correction your position would drift kilometres every day.
6. **Incognito mode does not make you anonymous** — It hides nothing from your provider, your employer or the site.
7. **The Turing test** — What it measures is deception, not intelligence.
8. **Why progress bars lie** — Progress indicators are mostly guessed, not measured.
9. **End-to-end encryption** — What it protects, and what stays visible anyway: the metadata.
10. **Why batteries age** — Chemical wear, and why charging to full accelerates it.
11. **Training versus inference in AI** — Two completely different processes, constantly confused.
12. **Why language models hallucinate** — They predict probabilities. They do not look anything up.
13. **The internet is not a cloud** — It is cables on the ocean floor, and sharks really do bite them.
14. **Bluetooth is named after a Viking king** — The logo is his initials written in runes.
15. **The first computer bug** — Grace Hopper and the moth in the relay. The story is almost true.
16. **Why video is compressed** — Keyframes, motion vectors, and why fast scenes turn to mush.
17. **Public-key cryptography** — How two strangers agree on a secret while everyone is listening.
18. **Why passwords are hashed** — And why a service that emails you your password has a problem.
19. **Capacitive touchscreens** — Why gloves do not work but your finger does.
20. **Placebo buttons** — Many pedestrian crossing and door-close buttons are not connected to anything.
21. **Why aeroplane mode exists** — The actual reason is not that the plane would crash.
22. **Random versus pseudorandom** — Computers cannot roll dice. How they do it anyway.
23. **Why SSDs die suddenly** — A different failure mode from hard drives, and without warning.
24. **Rubber duck debugging** — Explaining a problem out loud solves it. The reason is psychologically interesting.
25. **The history of the @ sign** — An accounting symbol made world-famous by a single decision in 1971.
